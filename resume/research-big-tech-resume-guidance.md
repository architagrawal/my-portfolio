# Official Google and Amazon resume guidance

Research date: 2026-09-12  
Scope: primary sources published by Google and Amazon only.

## Bottom line

Google provides the clearest official resume-writing formula:

> Accomplished **[X]**, as measured by **[Y]**, by doing **[Z]**.

In plain language: **result/impact → measurement → action/method**. Google also recommends concise, simple bullets, action words, metrics, role-specific tailoring, and a one-page resume for candidates with fewer than ten years of relevant experience.

Amazon does **not** appear to prescribe an official resume bullet formula or universal resume layout in its public hiring guidance. Amazon officially says its behavioral interviews focus on the **what** and **how** of an experience and the **why** behind decisions. It recommends STAR and data/metrics for interview responses. Thus, “what–why–how” is a useful way to develop the substance of a resume bullet, but it should not be presented as an official Amazon resume format.

## Google: official resume guidance

### Accomplishment bullets

Google's official resume-writing guide recommends:

- Begin the work-experience section with employer, position, and dates.
- Keep bullets clear, concise, and descriptive.
- Use the framework **“Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z].”**
- Focus on impact, action words, and metrics.
- Maintain roughly a **50/50 balance** between explaining role responsibilities and personal accomplishments or above-and-beyond contribution.
- Match relevant accomplishments to the job's responsibilities and minimum/preferred qualifications.

The same guide suggests questions that uncover a strong story: What value did the work create? What was the project's purpose? What did the candidate analyze, design, or execute? What tools did they use? Did productivity improve, and by how much?

Source: [Google, Resume writing for veterans — VetNet template (2021)](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/resume-writing-tips-for-veterans-2021.pdf), especially pp. 2–6. Although written for veterans, its resume structure and bullet guidance are stated generally and are directly reusable.

An older official Google resume handout similarly says experience should show impact rather than only responsibilities, recommends action verbs, and advises adding numbers **with context**. It asks what the candidate accomplished, for what purpose, whether they created or improved something, and how many people were affected.

Source: [Google, Resume Overview](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/resumetipshandout2016.pdf), especially p. 1.

### Length, structure, and formatting

Google's 2021 official guide recommends:

- PDF format.
- One to two pages maximum, **aiming for one page with fewer than ten years of relevant experience**.
- Clean, simple, consistent bullet points.
- Contact information; a physical address is unnecessary.
- No objective is required; an optional one- or two-sentence career/impact summary can help.
- For industry professionals: optional career/impact summary, work/relevant volunteer experience, activities/leadership, honors/awards, education/certifications, then interests.
- Work experience before education for industry professionals; education first may make sense for students.

Source: [Google, Resume writing for veterans — VetNet template (2021)](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/resume-writing-tips-for-veterans-2021.pdf), pp. 2–3.

The older official handout is stricter: one page, 10–12 point type, approximately 0.5-inch/1.27-cm margins, reverse chronological order, consistent headings and styling, and no first- or third-person prose. Its one-page rule appears oriented toward students/early-career candidates. The newer 2021 guide's one-to-two-page rule is the better general statement for experienced candidates.

Source: [Google, Resume Overview](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/resumetipshandout2016.pdf), p. 1.

Google's current application help sets a **2 MB maximum** for a resume upload, notes that application parsing can be incomplete, and advises candidates to verify parsed fields manually. It also limits candidates to three applications in a rolling 30-day window and advises focusing on the most suitable matches after reading qualifications carefully.

Source: [Google Careers Help, Apply for a job](https://support.google.com/googlecareers/answer/6095391?hl=en).

### Tailoring and narrative

Official Google sources support tailoring rather than a single generic resume:

- The 2021 guide says to represent relevant job qualifications through the accomplishments listed.
- A Google BOLD alumna reports tailoring her resume to the role and mirroring relevant language from the program. This is personal advice published by Google, not a formal hiring rule.

Sources: [Google, Resume writing for veterans — VetNet template (2021)](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/resume-writing-tips-for-veterans-2021.pdf), pp. 5–6; [Google Blog, How Hannah Frankl advocates for startups and inclusivity](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/life-at-google/how-hannah-frankl-advocates-startups-and-inclusivity/).

## Amazon: official guidance

### What Amazon officially says

Amazon's public SDE interview preparation says behavioral interviews examine:

- the **what** and **how** of the candidate's experiences;
- the **why** behind the candidate's decisions;
- memorable, specific examples involving success, challenges, risks, failures, and growth;
- metrics or data where applicable;
- alignment with Amazon Leadership Principles;
- structured answers using **STAR**: Situation, Task, Action, Result.

Source: [Amazon Jobs, SDE III / Senior SDE Interview Prep](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/sde-iii-interview-prep), “Behavioral interview” and “How to prepare.”

Amazon repeats STAR for student and graduate technical-role behavioral interviews and explicitly defines it as situation, task, action, and result.

Source: [Amazon Jobs, Interview prep for technical roles](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/university/additional-tech), “Behavioral interview.”

### What Amazon does not officially say

The reviewed Amazon hiring materials do not prescribe:

- a universal Amazon resume template;
- a page count for technical resumes;
- a mandatory bullet order such as “what–why–how”;
- STAR as a literal four-part format for every resume bullet.

Amazon's **what/how/why** and **STAR** advice is explicitly framed as interview preparation. Applying the thinking to resume bullets is reasonable, but it is an adaptation—not an official Amazon resume rule.

## Recommended synthesis for this resume

Use Google's X–Y–Z formula as the visible bullet structure and Amazon's what/how/why plus STAR as the hidden drafting process.

For each accomplishment, first collect:

1. **Context/problem:** What was failing, slow, risky, or strategically important?
2. **Why:** Why did it matter—to users, customers, reliability, cost, speed, or the business?
3. **Action/how:** What did Archit personally design, build, decide, or lead? What difficult technical choice distinguishes the work?
4. **Result:** What changed? Quantify scale, speed, adoption, quality, revenue, reliability, or people affected.

Then compress that material into one recruiter-scannable bullet, usually ordered as:

> **Action + artifact/system + impact/measurement + distinguishing method/context**

or, when the metric is the strongest hook:

> **Impact/measurement + action + distinguishing method**

Examples of the shape (not claims to copy without verification):

- Reduced transcript-analysis time from **4 hours to 15 minutes** by building [system/workflow], enabling [user or business outcome].
- Built [production system] serving **60,000+ students and 1,000+ faculty**, using [distinctive architecture] to solve [important reliability/product constraint].

This provides story without turning bullets into paragraphs. The sequence of bullets within each role can carry the larger narrative:

1. **Mission/ownership:** the most important system or problem owned.
2. **Scale/outcome:** the strongest measurable result.
3. **Technical judgment:** architecture, reliability, or ambiguity resolved.
4. **Leverage/leadership:** adoption, collaboration, reusable platform, mentoring, or organizational effect.

## Practical conclusion

For a candidate with under ten years of relevant experience, the safest big-tech baseline is a tailored, ATS-readable, reverse-chronological, one-page resume if the strongest relevant evidence fits legibly. A second page is defensible when necessary to preserve materially relevant work, but depth should not come from long bullets or exhaustive tool lists. The portfolio can retain the full case-study detail; the resume should select the proof most relevant to each target role.
